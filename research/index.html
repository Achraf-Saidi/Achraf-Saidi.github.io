Research

Statistics for reliable prediction.

My research interests lie at the intersection of statistical learning and machine learning, with a particular focus on calibration, survival analysis and predictive reliability for complex data.

I am a PhD researcher at UCLouvain within the Institute of Statistics, Biostatistics and Actuarial Sciences (ISBA), part of LIDAM. I deliberately keep ongoing doctoral work at a high level here until the corresponding results are ready to be public.

Research areas

Calibration Survival analysis Machine learning Biomedical data Methods

01 / Calibration

Predictive reliability

Accuracy alone is not enough when a model outputs probabilities, risks or scores that may inform scientific or clinical decisions. I am interested in how predicted quantities correspond to observed outcomes, how this agreement can be evaluated, and how reliability changes when data or observation mechanisms are complex.

02 / Survival analysis

Time-to-event data

Survival data introduce questions that ordinary regression does not: outcomes may be censored, follow-up varies between individuals, and prediction quality changes over time. I am interested in statistical and machine-learning methods that remain interpretable and assessable under these constraints.

03 / Machine learning

Flexible models, careful evaluation

My broader interests include machine learning and deep learning, representation learning and modern predictive systems. The part I find most interesting is often not the architecture itself, but what happens when these models meet real data: validation, uncertainty, calibration, distribution shift and reproducibility.

04 / Biomedical applications

Clinical, imaging & multi-omics data

I am interested in biomedical problems where data are heterogeneous and high-dimensional: clinical variables, cancer datasets, medical imaging and multi-omics measurements. My previous project work has included predictive modelling on biomedical data and deep-learning experiments for image segmentation and representation learning.

05 / Methods

Statistical and computational toolbox

Depending on the problem, my work draws on statistical modelling, Bayesian reasoning, optimization, machine learning, deep learning and reproducible scientific computing.
