A line that learns: regression and gradient descent

70 min · Reading & practice

How do we choose a line?

Section titled “How do we choose a line?”

We observe three pairs (xi,yi)(x_i,y_i)(xi​,yi​): (0,1)(0,1)(0,1), (1,3)(1,3)(1,3) and (2,5)(2,5)(2,5). The index iii labels observations from 1 to n=3n=3n=3. Predict an output using y^i=wxi+b\hat y_i=wx_i+by^​i​=wxi​+b. The symbol y^\hat yy^​ denotes a prediction, www a slope and bbb an intercept. Here w=2,b=1w=2,b=1w=2,b=1 fits every point. A learning procedure should recover parameters without being told this answer.

Prerequisites: derivatives, vectors and matrices and Python. Goals: construct least squares, derive gradients, calculate an update and define an honest evaluation.

Turning a preference into a function

Section titled “Turning a preference into a function”

The signed residual is ei=wxi+b−yie_i=wx_i+b-y_iei​=wxi​+b−yi​. Positive and negative residuals should not cancel artificially, so minimize their mean square, the MSE:

L(w,b)=1n∑i=1n(wxi+b−yi)2.L(w,b)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(wx_i+b-y_i)^2.L(w,b)=n1​∑i=1n​(wxi​+b−yi​)2.

The training loss LLL is a nonnegative real number. Squaring strongly penalizes large discrepancies, giving simple geometry but sensitivity to extreme observations. MSE has the squared units of yyy; its square root, RMSE, restores the original units.

Initially w=b=0w=b=0w=b=0, so residuals are −1,−3,−5-1,-3,-5−1,−3,−5 and L(0,0)=(1+9+25)/3=35/3L(0,0)=(1+9+25)/3=35/3L(0,0)=(1+9+25)/3=35/3. At w=2,b=1w=2,b=1w=2,b=1, residuals vanish and L=0L=0L=0, a global minimum because squares cannot be negative.

Differentiating with respect to two parameters

Section titled “Differentiating with respect to two parameters”

A partial derivative varies one parameter while holding the other fixed. By the chain rule, ∂ei2/∂w=2eixi\partial e_i^2/\partial w=2e_i x_i∂ei2​/∂w=2ei​xi​ and ∂ei2/∂b=2ei\partial e_i^2/\partial b=2e_i∂ei2​/∂b=2ei​. The symbol ∂\partial∂ denotes partial differentiation. Summing gives

∂L∂w=2n∑ieixi,∂L∂b=2n∑iei.\frac{\partial L}{\partial w}=\frac2n\sum_i e_i x_i,\qquad\frac{\partial L}{\partial b}=\frac2n\sum_i e_i.∂w∂L​=n2​∑i​ei​xi​,∂b∂L​=n2​∑i​ei​.

The gradient collects these derivatives into a vector. Under Euclidean geometry, it points toward greatest local increase. To reduce loss, try the opposite direction with a positive step size η\etaη, the learning rate:

wnew=w−η∂L∂w,bnew=b−η∂L∂b.w_{\mathrm{new}}=w-\eta\frac{\partial L}{\partial w},\qquad b_{\mathrm{new}}=b-\eta\frac{\partial L}{\partial b}.wnew​=w−η∂w∂L​,bnew​=b−η∂b∂L​.

Calculate both derivatives at the old parameters. Updating www before calculating the derivative with respect to bbb would produce a different procedure.

Implementing the update

Section titled “Implementing the update”

This is batch gradient descent: every update uses every observation. Stochastic descent uses one observation or a small batch and requires an analysis of gradient noise.

The matrix view and a direct solution

Section titled “The matrix view and a direct solution”

Let X∈Rn×2X\in\mathbb R^{n\times2}X∈Rn×2 contain a column of ones for the intercept and a column of xix_ixi​ values. With β=(b,w)T\beta=(b,w)^Tβ=(b,w)T and output vector y∈Rny\in\mathbb R^ny∈Rn, loss is ∥Xβ−y∥2/n\|X\beta-y\|^2/n∥Xβ−y∥2/n. Setting its gradient to zero gives the normal equations XTXβ=XTyX^TX\beta=X^TyXTXβ=XTy. Independent columns give a unique solution. Dependent columns can allow several parameter vectors to produce the same training predictions.

The full-rank formula (XTX)−1XTy(X^TX)^{-1}X^Ty(XTX)−1XTy expresses the solution, but explicitly computing an inverse is usually a poor numerical choice. Use numpy.linalg.lstsq or a QR/SVD solver. With scikit-learn:

In R, lm(y ~ x) fits the same model with an intercept. Statistical uncertainty requires additional assumptions. Minimizing squared errors does not require normally distributed errors; classical tests and intervals have their own assumptions.

Fitting is not generalization

Section titled “Fitting is not generalization”

These three points are synthetic and perfectly aligned. Zero training loss does not establish performance on new observations. A real project reserves test data, selects hyperparameters without consulting it, and compares against a constant baseline. Temporal or patient-level splits may be necessary. Fit every learned preprocessing transformation using training data alone.
