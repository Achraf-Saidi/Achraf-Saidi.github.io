## What are we looking for when we solve?

A subscription costs €5 plus €2 per session. How many sessions give a bill of €17? Let $x$ denote the number of sessions. The expression $5+2x$ describes a possible bill; the equation $5+2x=17$ imposes a condition. **Solving** means finding every allowed value of $x$ that makes it true.

Prerequisite: [numbers and fractions](/achraf-notebooks/en/foundations/numbers/). Goals: distinguish expressions, identities and equations; expand, factor and solve simple linear equations.

## Letters have roles

A **variable** represents a value that can change. An **unknown** is a value sought in a problem. A **parameter** stays fixed during one calculation but can vary between problems. In $ax+b=0$, $x$ may be the unknown and $a,b$ the parameters. Context determines the role, not the letter.

The notation $2x$ means $2\times x$. An expression such as $x^2+3x$ makes no assertion: it can be evaluated at a given $x$. An equation asserts equality for the values being sought. An **identity**, such as $(x+1)^2=x^2+2x+1$, holds for every real $x$.

## Preserve the balance

Starting from $5+2x=17$, subtract 5 from both sides to obtain $2x=12$. Divide both sides by 2 to obtain $x=6$. Substitution checks the result: $5+2\times6=17$.

Adding the same quantity to both sides preserves equivalence. Multiplication or division by the same **nonzero** number does too. The symbol $\iff$ means “if and only if”: the equations have the same solutions.

$$5+2x=17\iff2x=12\iff x=6.$$

Dividing by an expression that might equal zero can lose solutions. Dividing $x(x-1)=0$ by $x$ discards $x=0$. Instead, a product of real numbers equals zero if and only if at least one factor does. The solutions are 0 and 1.

## Expanding and factoring

Distributivity, $a(b+c)=ab+ac$, connects addition to multiplication. Thus $(x+2)(x+3)=x^2+3x+2x+6=x^2+5x+6$. Expanding turns a product into a sum. Factoring reverses that operation: $x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)$ reveals roots $-2$ and $-3$.

For the square of a sum:

$$(a+b)^2=(a+b)(a+b)=a^2+ab+ba+b^2=a^2+2ab+b^2.$$

The cross term $2ab$ will be essential when deriving the derivative of $x^2$. It also explains why $(a+b)^2\ne a^2+b^2$ in general.

## Restrictions and inequalities

In $1/(x-2)$, the value $x=2$ is forbidden. Record excluded values before manipulating an expression. Multiplying an inequality by a negative number reverses its direction: $-2x<6$ is equivalent to $x>-3$. Changing signs reflects positions on the number line and reverses their order.

Context may impose additional restrictions: session counts must be nonnegative integers. An algebraically valid negative solution would then be excluded from the practical problem.

## Calculate, then verify

```python
x = (17 - 5) / 2
assert 5 + 2 * x == 17
print(x)  # 6.0
```

Here `assert` checks an exact property of these small numbers. General floating-point calculations require a tolerance. A computer solver does not remove the need to identify the solution domain.

## Exercises

1. Solve $3(x-2)=12$ and check the result.
2. Solve $x^2=3x$ without losing a solution.
3. Simplify $(x^2-1)/(x-1)$ and specify its domain.

Factor in 2 and 3. Simplification does not remove the original domain restrictions.

<details>
<summary>Worked solutions</summary>



1. $x-2=4$, so $x=6$. Check: $3(6-2)=12$.
2. $x^2-3x=x(x-3)=0$, so $x=0$ or $x=3$. Dividing straight away by $x$ would lose 0.
3. $x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)$. For $x\ne1$, the quotient equals $x+1$. At $x=1$, the original expression remains undefined.
