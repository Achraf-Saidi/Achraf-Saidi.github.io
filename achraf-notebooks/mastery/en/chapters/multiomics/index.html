Multi-omics, multimodal representation and GNNs in health

Understand biological measurement, fuse modalities without leakage and know when a graph network is justified.

Method

Define the variables. Re-derive each equation. Reproduce the example independently. Test the code. Explain assumptions and limitations.

01 — Understand the measurements first

DNA stores genetic information; transcription generates RNA; translation produces proteins, with many intervening regulatory steps. Multi-omics data may contain genomic variants, transcript abundances, proteins, methylation and metabolites for a cohort. These matrices are not complete snapshots of an organism: assays measure molecules through experimental protocols with noise, bias and limits of detection.

RNA-seq read counts should not be treated as simple directly comparable concentrations: sequencing depth, composition and other measurement factors matter. Task-appropriate normalization and count modeling are needed. Proteomics may have non-random missingness linked to detection thresholds. Gene annotation, experimental batches and biological reference maps also change over time.

02 — Three fusion strategies

Early fusion concatenates suitably normalized feature tables, then fits a common model. It is simple but sensitive to scale, disparate dimensionality and missing modalities. Intermediate fusion learns modality-specific representations hᴿᴺᴬ=fᴿᴺᴬ(Xᴿᴺᴬ) and hᴾ=fᴾ(Xᴾ), then integrates them in a shared latent space. Contrastive or variational objectives may align representations. Late fusion combines separate predictions; this supports modularity but may miss fine interactions.

With n patients and tens of thousands of features, regularization, selection stability and simple baselines are central. A deep architecture does not automatically beat penalized regression when n≪p. Feature selection, normalization choices and threshold tuning must occur inside each training fold to prevent leakage.

03 — Constructing a biologically grounded graph

Different graphs express different assumptions: genes linked by interactions, protein networks, biological pathways and patient-similarity graphs are not interchangeable. In a gene graph, nodes may carry patient-specific expression measurements while edges come from an independent interaction network. This encodes an inductive bias that related genes can exchange information. However, curated interactions are not automatically causal relations, and annotation quality varies.

A basic graph convolution is H′=σ(D̃⁻¹ᐟ² Ã D̃⁻¹ᐟ² H W), where Ã=A+I adds self-loops, D̃ᵢᵢ=ΣⱼÃᵢⱼ contains degrees, H∈ℝᴺˣᶠ contains f features for N nodes, and W∈ℝᶠˣᶠ′ is learned. The output is N×f′. Normalized graph propagation gathers neighboring information; nonlinear activation σ adds expressive power. Other architectures use attention, heterogeneous relations and equivariance.

04 — Credible biomedical evaluation

Split by patient across train, validation and test; if a patient has multiple samples, keep them in the same group. Where possible, test transfer across independent centers, labs, time periods or cohorts. Batch effects must not unintentionally encode diagnosis. For survival prognosis, follow-up, censoring and competing risks complicate evaluation: C-index, censoring-adjusted Brier loss and fixed-horizon calibration answer different questions.

Build baselines before a GNN: individual modalities, Elastic Net after fusion, penalized survival models and late fusion. Report appropriate uncertainty, edge/modality/batch ablations and external validation. A tiny gain on one split does not justify a more complicated architecture. Public repositories such as TCGA/GDC have variable access requirements across modalities; consult official documentation rather than assuming all clinical records are downloadable without restriction.

06 — Reproducible code

This program runs with Python and NumPy on an ordinary computer. It is an educational laboratory, not a clinical or production system.

import numpy as np
# Toy three-gene interaction graph; illustrative, NOT a biological network.
A = np.array([[0, 1, 0],
              [1, 0, 1],
              [0, 1, 0]], dtype=float)
H = np.array([[1., 0.], [0., 1.], [2., 1.]])  # 3 genes × 2 features
W = np.array([[0.5, 0.3], [0.2, -0.1]])       # 2 features × 2 outputs
A_tilde = A + np.eye(3)
degree = A_tilde.sum(axis=1)
D_inv_sqrt = np.diag(degree**(-0.5))
P = D_inv_sqrt @ A_tilde @ D_inv_sqrt
H_next = np.maximum(P @ H @ W, 0.)           # ReLU
assert H_next.shape == (3, 2)
print(H_next)

07 — Solved exercise

How does a gene graph differ from a patient graph? Why does splitting two samples from the same person across training and test inflate performance? Design an ablation to test whether biological edges help.

Reveal the reasoning and solution

The nodes and edges encode different entities and relationships: genes versus patients. Multiple samples share personal characteristics, causing leakage. Compare the biological graph to an edgeless or controlled randomized graph at matched model capacity, ideally on external test cohorts.
